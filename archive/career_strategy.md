### 5. How do I package what I can do?

**The core asset:**
Most engineers don't have commercial skin. Most salespeople don't have technical depth. The value sits in the middle — someone who has lived the full arc of enterprise conversational AI (legacy chatbot platforms through to agentic workflows) and has the customer-facing, GTM layer on top of it. That combination is genuinely rare.

**Primary positioning:**
Enterprise Agentic CX — helping enterprises navigate the move from legacy conversational AI to production-ready agentic systems. Not a pure engineer, not a pure consultant. A technical advisor who can design, communicate, and implement.

**One core profile, adapted by door:**
The same track record and positioning, with emphasis shifting depending on the opportunity. Same room, different entrance.

---

**Five problem-centred profiles:**

**Profile 1: "We can't tell if our AI is any good"**
Companies build conversational AI, deploy it, and have no reliable way to measure whether it's working. Design the measurement systems — what to test, how to test it, what the results mean, and what to do when something fails.
*Strongest evidence: NLU evaluation for enterprise clients, eval tool (TRAIL benchmark), GEPA integration*

**Profile 2: "We built a chatbot three years ago and now we need something that can actually do things"**
Most enterprise contact centre AI is old-style intent classification that can't handle complexity. Lived both the old and new world — can redesign the system without throwing away what works or traumatising the operations team.
*Strongest evidence: Full arc from rule-based to NLU to LLM to agents, Avast CX operations background, HumanFirst delivery*

**Profile 3: "Our AI works in the demo but falls apart in real conversations"**
The gap between a controlled test and real customer interactions is where most projects fail. Understands why conversations go wrong — from the NLU side, the prompt side, and the operational side.
*Strongest evidence: NLU optimisation, conversational analysis, HumanFirst client delivery*

**Profile 4: "We deployed AI six months ago and we have no idea what's happening inside it"**
No visibility into failure modes, no way to catch when quality drops, no feedback loop from production back into the system. Builds the monitoring and review processes that make it manageable.
*Strongest evidence: Eval tool, LangSmith tracing, trace analysis work*

**Profile 5: "We bought AI tools but our teams aren't actually using them"**
Technology deployed, behaviour unchanged. Designs the training and workflow changes that bridge the gap between what the tool can do and what people actually do with it.
*Strongest evidence: Enterprise AI training programs at HumanFirst and CDI*

### What structure actually means

What to look for in a role and manager — not "regular check-ins and clear goals" but specifically:

- **Knowing what good looks like** — not just the goal, but what a good piece of work looks like versus a mediocre one, so self-assessment is possible
- **Someone to think with, not report to** — a manager who engages with the substance of the work, challenges thinking, and helps identify whether focus is in the right place
- **Clear scope** — knowing what is yours to own and what isn't; open-ended remits where everything is potentially your problem are the enemy of focused work
- **Short feedback loops** — regular enough contact to catch drift early, not quarterly reviews that find problems too late
- **Strategic context** — a manager who connects day-to-day work to the broader picture, explaining why certain things matter more than others right now, not just what to do

**How to say this in an interview:**
*"I do my best work when I have a clear definition of what success looks like, a manager who engages substantively with the work rather than just tracking status, and enough regular contact to course-correct quickly if priorities shift. I also find it valuable when a manager helps connect the work to the broader strategic picture — not just what to do but why it matters relative to everything else."*

---

### Key question to ask in every interview

"How does the team define success for this role in the first 90 days, and how is that reviewed?"

A company that answers this clearly and specifically is one worth joining. A company that says "we're flexible, you'll figure it out" is a warning sign — it means no structure, no clear outcomes, no feedback loop.
