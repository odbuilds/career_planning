# intv-prep

Generate a comprehensive interview prep document.

## Invocation

The user invokes this skill as `/intv-prep [URL or pasted JD]`.

- **URL provided:** `/intv-prep https://jobs.lever.co/company/role-id`
- **JD pasted inline:** `/intv-prep [full JD text]`
- **Nothing provided:** prompt the user — *"Paste the JD or drop a link."* — and wait before proceeding.

## Profile Sources

Before starting research, load these files to personalise all output:

1. Read `/home/ubuntu/source/career_planning/jobpilot/profile.yaml` — skills, positioning, experience
2. Select the most relevant CV from `/home/ubuntu/source/career_planning/cvs/` based on role type:

   - Consulting/advisory roles → `cv_ai_consulting.md`

   - Default → `cv_agentic.md`
3. Read `/home/ubuntu/source/career_planning/intv/interview_strategies.md` — named strategies and tactics

## Research Pipeline

Run each step in order. Print a one-line status before each step (e.g. `[1/4] Analysing JD...`).

### Step 1 — JD Analysis

From the JD text (fetched via WebFetch if a URL was provided), extract:

- **Company name** and **role title**
- **The one core problem** this hire is meant to solve — one sentence, specific, not a job description summary. Ask: what would go wrong for this company if this role stayed unfilled for 12 months?
- **3 key skills to evidence** — the capabilities the JD signals matter most, ranked by emphasis
- **Named hiring manager** if present in the JD
- **Startup flag** — is this a startup (Series C or earlier, or <500 employees)? Note for Step 4.

### Step 2 — Company Research

Run WebSearch queries:
- `"[company name]" what they do customers product`
- `"[company name]" business model revenue`
- `"[company name]" site:crunchbase.com OR site:linkedin.com/company`

Synthesise:
- Who they are and what they sell
- Their ideal and current customer profile
- The core problem they solve and their differentiation
- Company size, stage, and key facts

### Step 3 — Key People Research

For each of CEO, CTO, and named hiring manager (if known), run:
- `"[person name]" "[company name]" interview OR podcast OR talk`
- `"[person name]" "[company name]" strategy OR vision OR challenges`

Synthesise per person:
- Background and tenure
- What they've said publicly about strategy, priorities, and challenges
- Anything directly relevant to the role being hired for

### Step 4 — Recent News & Company Signals

Run WebSearch queries:
- `"[company name]" news 2025 OR 2026`
- `"[company name]" funding OR investment OR raised 2025 OR 2026`
- `"[company name]" product launch OR partnership OR acquisition 2025 OR 2026`

If startup flag is set, also run:
- `"[company name]" site:crunchbase.com funding rounds`
- `"[company name]" pivot OR layoffs OR restructure 2025 OR 2026`

Synthesise: recent product moves, funding status, any pivot signals or strategic shifts.

## Pause Point

After Step 4, stop and prompt the user with exactly this message (substituting found values):

---
*Research complete. Before I write the prep doc, I need a few things:*

*1. **Hiring manager's LinkedIn URL** — required. (If you don't know who it is, paste the company LinkedIn page and I'll work from that.)*
*2. **Any posts, comments, or content from them** you want me to factor in — optional, paste directly*
*3. **Any feedback from a previous round** with this company — optional*

*[Checking intv/ for a prior interview file from [company name]...]*

If a file matching the company name exists in `/home/ubuntu/source/career_planning/intv/`, report it:
*Found prior file: `intv/[filename]` — should I reference it? (yes/no)*

If no prior file exists:
*No prior file found for [company name].*

---

Wait for the user's response before continuing.

Once received:
- If a LinkedIn URL was provided: fetch it with WebFetch and extract recent posts, current role, stated priorities, and language patterns
- If posts were pasted: read them directly
- If previous round feedback was provided: note it for use in sections 5, 6, and 12
- If user confirmed a prior file: read it with Read tool

## Document Generation

Write the output file to `/home/ubuntu/source/career_planning/intv/YYYY-MM-DD-[company-slug].md` where:
- `YYYY-MM-DD` = today's date
- `[company-slug]` = company name lowercased, spaces replaced with hyphens (e.g. `the-economist`)

The document must contain all 12 sections below. Each section heading uses `##`. Use the full depth of research gathered — do not summarise shallowly.

---

### Section 1: Company Snapshot

Header:
```
# [Company] — [Role Title]
**Date:** [today's date]
**Stage:** [interview stage if known, else "First interview"]
```

Then a dense reference block covering: founded, stage, size, funding, product, customers, business model, key facts. This is for skimming the morning of the interview.

---

### Section 2: The One Thing

State the single core problem this hire is meant to solve in **one plain sentence**. This is the frame for the entire interview.

Then list the **3 key skills to evidence**, each with a one-line explanation of why it matters for this specific role.

---

### Section 3: Key People

One subsection per person (CEO, CTO, hiring manager if distinct). For each:
- Role, background, tenure
- What they've said publicly about strategy and challenges
- What to know before being in the room with them

---

### Section 4: Hiring Manager Intel

If LinkedIn data was provided, synthesise:
- What they're focused on right now
- The language and framing they use (quote specific phrases where possible)
- Any stated frustrations or challenges
- How to mirror their register without losing Oliver's voice

If no LinkedIn data was provided, write: *"[No LinkedIn data provided — research manually before the interview]"*

---

### Section 5: Interview Strategy

Recommend one named strategy from `intv/interview_strategies.md`. Include:
- Which strategy and why it fits this specific role and company moment
- How to open with it in the first 2 minutes
- How to sustain it across different question types
- If previous round feedback was provided: whether to shift strategy or reinforce it, with reasoning

---

### Section 6: Handling Weaknesses

Cross-reference Oliver's profile (profile.yaml + selected CV) against the JD requirements. For each meaningful gap:
- Name the gap plainly (one line)
- Suggest a specific framing that acknowledges it without undermining confidence
- Provide a pivot to an adjacent strength using a real example from Oliver's background

If previous round feedback named specific concerns, address each one explicitly with a suggested reframe.

---

### Section 7: Tell Me About Yourself

A scripted answer (2–3 minutes spoken, ~350 words) that:
- Opens with identity/positioning, not chronology ("I build X for companies that need Y")
- Is anchored to the one core problem from Section 2
- References 2–3 specific, credible pieces of work from Oliver's background
- Closes with a clear "why this role, why now" bridge

Write it as a first-person script, not bullet points.

---

### Section 8: Why This Company / Why Now

A scripted answer (~150 words) to "why us?" that:
- Opens with something specific from the research (a product decision, a recent funding round, something the CEO said publicly)
- Avoids generic praise ("I love your mission")
- Connects the company's current moment to Oliver's specific interests and trajectory

Write it as a first-person script.

---

### Section 9: Ten Expected Questions

List 10 questions most likely to come up for this role. For each:

**Q[N]: [Question]**
*Why they're asking:* [the underlying concern in one sentence]
*Format:* [STAR or PCR]
*Answer:*
[Full scripted answer, customised with Oliver's specific experience and this company's context. Minimum 150 words per answer. No bullet-point answers — write prose the way Oliver would actually speak it.]

---

### Section 10: Questions to Ask Them

List 5 questions Oliver can ask the interviewer. For each, tag it:
- **[Strategic]** — advances the chosen strategy posture
- **[Diagnostic]** — surfaces information useful for evaluating the role
- **[Signal]** — demonstrates homework and thinking

Format each as: the question itself, then one line on why it's worth asking.

---

### Section 11: 30-60-90 Day Sketch

A rough outline customised to this specific role and company:

**Days 1–30:** [what to orient around, what to listen for, what not to do yet]
**Days 31–60:** [first concrete moves, early wins to target]
**Days 61–90:** [longer-horizon bets, how success would be measured]

Write each phase as 3–5 sentences of prose, not a bullet list.

---

### Section 12: Watch-Outs

List any red flags to monitor during the interview. For each, suggest how to approach it:

- **Seniority mismatch** — if the role seems to be pitched junior relative to Oliver's level, or vice versa: how to probe and how to respond
- **Role ambiguity** — if the mandate is unclear, there are multiple stakeholders, or success metrics are vague: what questions to ask
- **Company signals** — anything from the research that warrants caution (funding concerns, pivot risk, cultural friction, high churn in this function)
- **Prior round carryover** — if a prior interview file was referenced, any unresolved concerns that may resurface

Only include watch-outs that are actually warranted by the research. Do not pad with generic interview advice.
