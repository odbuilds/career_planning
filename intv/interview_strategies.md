# Interview Strategies & Tactics

## Strategies
*The overarching posture that frames the whole interview*

### 2-Way Partnership
You treat the interview as a mutual evaluation — you're assessing them as much as they're assessing you. Signals confidence, sets peer-level tone, and filters for roles where you'll actually thrive.

### Solution Provider
You walk in having already diagnosed their likely problems and with a point of view on how to solve them. Positions you as already useful before you're hired, not as someone hoping to be given a chance.

### Consultant Engagement
You run the interview like a client discovery session — asking diagnostic questions, reflecting back what you're hearing, building a shared picture of the problem. Shifts the dynamic from interrogation to collaboration.

### 30-60-90 Day Plan
You've thought through what you'd actually do in the role across the first three months and can articulate it clearly. Demonstrates strategic thinking, reduces hiring risk, and shows you've taken the role seriously.

### The Challenger
You respectfully push back on assumptions, surface tensions the interviewer may not have named, and offer a perspective they haven't heard. Works best for senior roles where the company needs someone with genuine judgment, not just someone who agrees well.

## Tactics
*Deployed within your chosen strategy*

### Proactive Redirection
You surface a likely doubt the interviewer has before they raise it, then reframe it on your terms. Gets ahead of hesitation rather than letting it sit unspoken and unresolved.

### Strategic Vulnerability
You name a genuine area of growth honestly, in a way that builds trust rather than undermining confidence. Shows self-awareness and makes your strengths more credible by contrast.

### Evidence-First Storytelling
You open answers with the outcome, then work backwards through what you did and why. Inverts the typical build-up-to-punchline structure and keeps the interviewer anchored in results.

### Trojan Horse
You embed your differentiating qualities inside stories rather than claiming them directly. More persuasive than self-description because the interviewer concludes it themselves.

### Peer Framing
You engage as someone with opinions and standards, not as a candidate making a case. Especially effective with technical interviewers who respond to substance over polish.

### Mirroring
You adapt your register to each interviewer — more technical with engineers, more strategic with executives, more outcome-focused with hiring managers. Signals versatility and social intelligence without losing your voice.
