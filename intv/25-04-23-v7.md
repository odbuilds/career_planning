## Company Research

### What V7 Is
V7 was founded in 2018 by Alberto Rizzoli and Simon Edwardsson. Originally a computer vision / data labeling company (V7 Darwin), they pivoted in 2025 to centre their commercial strategy on **V7 Go** — an AI agent platform for document-heavy knowledge work in regulated industries. Total funding: ~$50M ($33M Series A, Nov 2022). ~21–100 employees. Named 8th fastest-growing startup in 2024 (Sifted).

**Two products:**
- **V7 Darwin** — AI data annotation/labeling platform for training vision models. The heritage product.
- **V7 Go** — No-code multi-modal AI agent platform. The commercial bet. Processes tens of millions of documents across finance, insurance, and real estate.

### V7 Go: What It Does
A no-code platform where enterprise customers build and deploy AI agents that read, extract, reason across complex documents — contracts, claims, leases, underwriting packs. Users describe what they need in natural language; V7 Go determines the optimal workflow. Connects to existing systems via APIs.

**Accuracy claim:** 95–99.9% on 1-shot benchmarks vs 80–95% for leading LLMs and 80–99% for RPA/IDP tools.

**Key differentiator:** Privacy/security-first — V7 does not train on customer documents. Operates within the customer's security perimeter. Critical for regulated industries.

**Published outcomes:** 92% policy clause extraction accuracy; 75% reduction in underwriting admin work; 8x faster property analysis; 35% productivity increase in first month (Centerline); 40% reduction in workflow costs for one customer; a V7 deployment that would've cost $2M and 18 months to build in-house went live in six weeks.

## Interview Strategy

**Primary:** Solution Provider + Consultant Engagement

Walk in having already diagnosed their customers' problems. In discovery/POC conversations, run the room like a consultant — ask diagnostic questions, reflect back what you hear, build a shared picture before proposing anything. This signals you're already useful before day one.

**Supporting tactics:**
- **Evidence-First Storytelling** — lead with outcomes, work backwards (e.g. "We had a client live in 6 weeks who expected 18 months. Here's how...")
- **Trojan Horse** — embed that you understand regulated industries (eval, quality loops, auditability) inside your stories rather than claiming it
- **Peer Framing** — engage as someone with opinions about what good agent design looks like, not as a candidate hoping to impress

**Opening posture:** You're not looking for a job, you're evaluating whether this is the right place to do the most interesting work of your career in AI delivery. Hold that frame.

## Likely Interview Questions & Angles

### AI Delivery / Technical
1. **"Walk us through an LLM project you've delivered end-to-end with a customer."**
   → Lead with outcome. How fast, what accuracy, what impact. Then back into the build — prompt design, evaluation, iteration.

2. **"How do you ensure accuracy when deploying LLMs on sensitive documents?"**
   → This is their core selling point. Show you think about evals, human-in-the-loop checkpoints, hallucination mitigation, and citation/traceability. Mention your HumanFirst eval work.

3. **"How would you approach a POC for an insurance underwriting client?"**
   → Structured discovery first. Identify the specific document type and extraction task. Define accuracy targets before building. Run a constrained pilot, measure against baseline, then expand.

4. **"What's your experience with prompt engineering in production?"**
   → Go beyond "I write prompts." Talk about versioning, regression testing, structured outputs, few-shot calibration.

5. **"How do you handle a situation where the model keeps failing on edge cases?"**
   → Triage the failure mode. Is it the prompt, the model, or the data? Build targeted evals. Loop in human review for low-confidence cases. Escalate to eng if it's a platform limit.

### Customer-Facing / GTM
6. **"How do you manage 10 concurrent projects without anything falling through?"**
   → Visibility systems, clear status cadence with customers, ruthless triage. Know which are at risk vs. humming. Communicate proactively, don't wait to be chased.

7. **"Tell us about a time you turned a failing POC into a win."**
   → Recalibrating scope mid-flight, resetting expectations, finding the smaller provable win that unlocks confidence.

8. **"How do you identify expansion opportunities within an account?"**
   → Listen for pain adjacent to what you're solving. Ask about the downstream consumer of the output. Who else in the org is doing something similar manually?

9. **"How do you bridge the gap between what sales promises and what's technically feasible?"**
   → Honest early scoping. Build a habit of being in the room for discovery. Create a shared definition of done before the contract is signed.

10. **"How do you upskill quickly on an industry you don't know?"**
    → Be specific: read the regulator's handbook, talk to the customer's internal SME, find the 2–3 document types that are highest volume, focus there first.

### Culture / Fit
11. **"Why V7 over other AI companies?"**
    → Specifics: regulated verticals with real accuracy requirements (not chatbots), the land-and-expand motion, being the technical backbone of a small GTM team where your work is directly visible.

12. **"What does ownership mean to you in a role like this?"**
    → Don't wait to be told there's a problem. Surface issues before customers do. Treat your accounts as if they're your own business outcomes.

13. **"What's a mistake you've made in a technical customer engagement?"**
    → Use strategic vulnerability here. Pick something real, show the lesson, show how you'd do it differently.
